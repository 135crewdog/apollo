What Apollo is

Apollo is a personal KC-135 aircrew training tracker. You tap what you did on a sortie, in the sim or in a ground class, and the rows land in a Google Sheet that you own. A small script inside that sheet works out currency, due dates and volume for every event in your column of the RTM and writes an Individual Training Summary. The app shows you that summary, overdue first.

Three things to know before you start:

  • Your data stays in your Google Drive. Nobody else, including the person who sent you this guide, can see your records. The app is a static web page with no server behind it; every device talks only to its owner's sheet.
  • Every date is Zulu. The app, the sheet and the summary all use the UTC date. A late-evening sortie may belong to tomorrow's Zulu date.
  • It is an unofficial tool. Your training office's records are the record of truth. Apollo helps you see what is coming; it does not replace Puckboard or your ITS.

What you need

  • A Google account with Drive.
  • Any device with a modern browser: phone, tablet or computer. Safari and Chrome both work.
  • About fifteen minutes, once. After setup, logging a sortie takes about as long as it does in Puckboard.

One-time setup

Eight steps. Steps 2 to 7 happen inside Google Sheets and the Apps Script editor, easiest on a computer; step 8 is on the device you will log from.

  1. Make your copy of the template

    The template holds the four tabs with their headers, the UTC time zone, and the RTM events for one crew position and column. Google will ask you to make a copy; the copy is yours and lands in your Drive.

    Copy the template: Pilot, MP / FTL A

    Based on the FY25 KC-135 RTM Change 2, 30 June 2025. The same note is on cell A1 of each config tab in the copy. If your column differs, edit the two config tabs in your copy afterwards.

    More

    Your copy has four tabs. Training Log is where every accomplishment lands, one row each. Ground Training Config and Flying Training Config list the events Apollo tracks, in the RTM's own words: a Task ID, a name, and the currency or frequency label, plus volume and sim-credit percentage for flying events. Individual Training Summary is written by the script; never type in it.

    You may rename the file and reorder or recolor the config rows. Do not rename the tabs or the header cells, and do not convert any tab to a Table: the script finds everything by those names and cannot write through a Table.

    To track a different column of the RTM, change the labels and numbers on the config tabs to match it. Labels the script understands: Monthly, Quarterly, Semi-Annual, Annual, Biennial, Triennial, any N Years, N Months or N Days, and PCS, As Required or N/A for events with no due date. Anything else shows CHECK LABEL in the summary so you can fix it.

  2. Open the script editor

    In your copy, choose Extensions → Apps Script. A project opens with one file, Code.gs, holding a stub function.

    More

    The editor opens in a new browser tab and is already tied to your sheet; you never have to connect the two. If the Extensions menu has no Apps Script entry, your Google account is a work or school account whose administrator has switched scripts off. Make the copy with a personal Google account instead.

  3. Paste Code.gs

    Click into Code.gs, select everything, delete it, and paste the contents of Code.js from the code section below. Save with Ctrl/Cmd+S.

    More

    Use the Copy button beside Code.js on this page, click anywhere inside the editor, press Ctrl+A (Cmd+A on a Mac) to select the stub, then paste. The first line of the file should be a comment starting /**. An orange dot on the file name means unsaved; it disappears when you save.

  4. Add rules.gs

    Click the + next to Files, choose Script, and name it rules. Delete the stub it contains and paste the contents of rules.js from below. Save.

    More

    The editor adds the .gs ending itself, so type only rules. When you are done the Files list shows exactly two entries, Code.gs and rules.gs. Code.gs talks to the sheet and the app; rules.gs holds the training rules, so that an RTM change is a config edit rather than a code change.

  5. Set your token

    Click the gear (Project Settings) in the left bar, scroll to Script Properties, and click Add script property. Property name APOLLO_TOKEN. Value: a secret of your own made of lowercase letters and digits only. Eight or more characters is plenty; it guards a training log, not a bank account. Click Save script properties.

    Other characters have broken the link between app and sheet before, so stick to letters and digits.

    More

    The token is a password that the app presents to your sheet on every sync, so that a stranger who guesses your web app URL still cannot read or write your log. A couple of words and some digits is a good shape. You can change it later in the same place; afterwards run Apollo → Connect device again so the app learns the new one. You never type it into the app by hand; the Connect device link carries it.

  6. Run it once to grant permissions

    Back in the editor, pick refresh in the function dropdown next to Run and click Run. Google asks you to authorize the script. If it warns that the app is unverified, choose Advanced, then Go to Apollo. When the run finishes, your sheet's summary tab is filled in.

    More

    The screens come in this order: choose your Google account; a page saying Google hasn't verified this app, where you click Advanced and then Go to Apollo (unsafe); then a list of what the script wants, which is to edit your spreadsheets and connect to an external service, where you click Allow. "Unsafe" and "unverified" appear because the script is your own copy that Google has never reviewed. You are authorizing your own script to edit your own sheet, and nothing else.

    When the run finishes, the editor's Execution log shows "Execution completed", and your sheet's Individual Training Summary tab lists every event with Overdue set on all of them, since the log is still empty.

  7. Deploy the web app

    Click Deploy → New deployment, click the gear next to Select type and choose Web app. Set Execute as: Me and Who has access: Anyone. Click Deploy. You do not need to copy the URL; the next step picks it up for you.

    More

    A deployment is the published copy of your script that the app talks to over the internet. Execute as Me means it runs under your account, which is what lets it write to your sheet. Anyone means anyone who has both the URL and your token can call it; the token is what keeps it yours. The URL is long and random; you never need to copy it, because the Connect device link carries it. Later, when the code changes, you come back to Deploy → Manage deployments and publish a new version of this same deployment, which keeps the URL.

  8. Connect your device

    Reload the sheet in your browser. An Apollo menu appears. Choose Apollo → Connect device. The dialog shows a link that opens the app with your sheet's connection filled in. Open that link on the device you will log from, or click Open on this device if that is the one. The app opens connected and shows your status. Then add it to your home screen: on iPhone or iPad Share → Add to Home Screen, on Android the install prompt or the browser menu's Install app, on a computer the browser's install option or a bookmark.

    That link contains your token. Send it only to yourself.

    More

    The link is the app's address with your web app URL and token tucked after a #. That part never leaves your device, so the server that hosts the app never sees your token. Getting the link to a device: AirDrop, a message to yourself, an email, or a notes app all work. On the device, the app opens, saves the connection, removes the token from the address bar, and syncs.

    Adding to the home screen makes Apollo open full-screen like an installed app and keeps working with no signal. On iPhone and iPad it must be done from Safari. You can connect as many devices as you like; each one gets the same link.

Using Apollo

Log

Pick Flight, Sim or Ground. Flight needs a mission number; the date defaults to today's Zulu date. Then find each event in the list, by name or by Task ID, and tap + once per time you did it. Three landings are three taps. Save writes one row per tap into your sheet. If you have no signal, the rows wait on the device and go when you do; the Status screen says how many are waiting.

More

A worked example: you flew mission 0123 today, did two landings, one instrument approach and one air refueling. On Log, choose Flight, type 0123, leave the date, tap + twice on Landing, once on Approach-Instrument, once on Tanker Air-to-Air Refueling, and Save. Four rows go into your sheet, each with the mission number and the date. In Sim, events with no sim credit are hidden, because logging them there would count for nothing. Switching between Flight, Sim and Ground clears the taps so a row never lands under the wrong kind.

Status

Your events, overdue first, then by due date. The badge color is the same as in the sheet: gray is overdue, red is due within 30 days, orange within 60, yellow within 90. Under each event: when you last did it, and for volume events how many of the year's requirement you have so far.

More

Due dates come from the label on your config tab, read the way the RTM reads them. Monthly, Quarterly and Semi-Annual events are due at the end of the period after the one you last did them in, with quarters and halves aligned to the fiscal year. Annual, Biennial and Triennial events are due on 30 September one, two or three fiscal years on. "N Months" and "N Days" count from the date itself. PCS, As Required and N/A have no due date and never show overdue.

Volume counts only this fiscal year, 1 October to 30 September. Sim rows count toward volume up to the event's sim-credit percentage of the requirement; "sim credit available" is how many more sim accomplishments can still count. An event is marked overdue when its due date has passed or when it has never been logged at all.

Settings

The gear in the top corner. Here you can see when the app last synced, switch the look between Light, Dark and Auto, and clear the app's local data from the device. Clearing never touches your sheet.

Your records

Backfill from your ITS

A new sheet knows nothing, so every event shows overdue. Fix that once by typing your history into the Training Log tab of your sheet: the Training ID in column A, the date in column B, and for flying events something in column C. Apollo does not care what the mission number on an imported row says; it is a note to your future self that the row came from your ITS rather than a sortie, so write something plain such as ITS import 6 Oct 26. Ground events leave column C blank.

How many rows per event: currency needs one, the most recent date. Volume needs one per accomplishment, but only for this fiscal year, since 1 October. So for an event you last did before 1 October, one row is enough. For an event done this fiscal year, enter as many rows as your ITS counts for it; if it shows three landings this year and only the latest date, enter that date three times.

More

Why the difference: the summary's due date comes from the latest row, so one row sets it. The volume column counts rows dated in the current fiscal year, so each accomplishment needs its own row, exactly as it would if you had logged the sortie in the app. Rows dated before 1 October affect nothing but currency, which is why last year's totals do not need re-creating.

Your ITS gives you the last date and this year's count per event. A quick way through it: for each event, type the last date once; then, for events with a count above one this year, copy that row until the count matches. Afterwards run Apollo → Refresh and compare the summary with the ITS. The Overdue column should agree with it, and each volume event's "Volume Accomplished" should match the ITS count.

The sheet is the record

You can add, fix or delete rows in the Training Log by hand at any time. The summary recalculates whenever the sheet is edited, opened, or synced. Type dates any way Sheets recognizes, such as 2026-10-05 or 5 Oct 2026; the column shows them as yyyy-mm-dd. Avoid all-numeric dates like 05/10/2026, which the sheet reads month-first.

The log check

After every refresh the sheet checks the log and shows a small message if a row needs attention: a Training ID that is not in either config tab, a date it cannot read, a date in the future, or a mission number that looks like SIM but is not exactly SIM. The same list appears on the app's Status screen. The check reports; it never changes your rows.

More

A row with an unknown Training ID or an unreadable date is ignored by the summary, so a typo there makes an event look overdue when it is not. A future date or a near-miss on SIM is still counted, but flagged so you can look. What the check cannot catch: a plausible wrong date, a wrong but valid ID, or a row typed twice, which the sheet treats as two accomplishments. Those need a human reading the log once.

Two things not to do

  • Do not use Format → Convert to table on any tab. A Google Sheets Table takes over the header row and the script cannot write through it. Colors, widths and number formats are fine.
  • Do not rename tabs or header cells. The script finds everything by those names.

Updates

The app updates itself. When a new version is published, your device fetches it the next time you open the app and shows it the time after that. The version number is at the bottom of Settings.

The script updates when you paste it. Your sheet's script is your own copy, so when the script changes you repeat two steps: paste the current Code.js and rules.js from the code section over your two files and save, then Deploy → Manage deployments → pencil → Version: New version → Deploy. Your URL and token do not change. Skipping the new-version step is the most common mistake: the sheet runs the new code while the app still gets the old one.

Troubleshooting

Settings says "Bad token"
The token in the app differs from the Script Property. Use Connect device again rather than typing. If the property itself has symbols in it, change it to lowercase letters and digits and redo Connect device.
"Unexpected reply. Check the web app URL and that access is set to Anyone."
The deployment is not reachable. In Manage deployments, confirm Execute as Me and Who has access Anyone, then redo Connect device.
The summary tab's columns are in a different order after the app syncs
The deployed web app is on an older version than the editor. Deploy a new version, then Apollo → Refresh.
The summary tab shows "Column 1, Column 2…" or is empty
A Table was put on that tab. The script rebuilds it on the next refresh; do not convert it to a Table again.
The app shows "No events" or an old summary
Open Settings and tap Sync now. If you have no signal, it will sync when you do.
The home-screen icon did not change after an update
iPhone and iPad copy the icon when you add the app and never refresh it. Remove it from the home screen and add it again from Safari. Android and desktop browsers update it on their own.
A row I typed is ignored
Read the log check message after Apollo → Refresh. Usually the Training ID has a typo or the date cell holds text the sheet did not recognize as a date.
